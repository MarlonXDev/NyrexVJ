# Changelog

## 0.31.0 - 2026-11-24

- GPU video pipeline audit: the whole video path was examined (FFmpeg, the hardware interfaces D3D11VA, DXVA2, NVDEC, QSV and VA-API, HAP, DXV to HAP) and no route was changed, so nothing plays differently. Where the decoder is on the graphics card the picture still takes one copy and one upload and is converted on the graphics card, without a read back and without a conversion on the processor; HAP still goes up compressed. Zero copy (D3D11 into OpenGL, NVDEC through CUDA, VA-API through DMA-BUF) was **not** built on purpose: all three need a real graphics card and driver to try, and code written blind for a driver can crash a show. [ADR 0024](docs/adr/0024-video-pipeline-audit-and-measurement.md) gives the reasons and `docs/MEDIA.md` has the table of routes and a checklist for a computer with a real graphics card.
- HDR and new numbers in the monitor: a video with a PQ or HLG transfer function is marked `HDR video (PQ)` or `HDR video (HLG)` in the monitor, the Clip window and the Library tooltip with the note that it is shown without tone mapping, so colours look flat (detected, not converted; tone mapping belongs to colour management). The video tooltips also show the time from opening a video to its first picture and, for the upload, the 95th percentile and the largest of the last 120 uploads next to the mean; `--bench-video` writes them as `firstPictureMs`, `transfer`, `uploadP95Ms` and `uploadMaxMs`. 5 new texts in all seven translations.
- Tests, measurements and documents: nine new colour space test clips (BT.601 limited, BT.709 full, BT.2020 limited and full at 10 bit, BT.2020 4:4:4 at 10 bit, PQ, HLG, 4:2:2 at 10 bit, 4:4:4 at 8 bit, 10 to 13 kB each) are compared with FFmpeg's own conversion in `nvj_media_tests` (1,294 checks) and on a real OpenGL context in `--video-selftest`, and each test also requires that the wrong matrix gives a clearly larger difference. The 4:4:4 clips differ by 0.002 of 255, so matrix and range are exact; 10 bit 4:2:0 differs by about 3 because of the chroma filter. `--video-selftest tests/data` exited with 1 because a DXV clip correctly takes the processor route and the check expected the graphics card route; that check is fixed and the self test now runs in the Linux CI when the FFmpeg libraries are there. All 29 tests, `--video-selftest`, `--live-selftest`, `--shader-selftest`, `--plugin-selftest`, `--output-selftest` and `scripts/check_i18n.py` pass on Linux. Measured with the Mesa software renderer: the new build against the old build, alternating, three runs each, shows no difference outside the spread (HAP 1080p 4.1 to 4.4 against 4.0 to 4.2 percent of one core, H.264 1080p 15.9 to 16.8 against 15.5 to 16.5, HEVC 1080p 30.4 to 31.0 against 30.3 to 30.9). New and changed documents: ADR 0024, `docs/MEDIA.md`, `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`, `docs/DECISIONS.md` and `docs/ROADMAP.md`.
- Known limits: nothing was run on a real graphics card, with a hardware decoder, on Windows at run time or next to VRChat, so there is no figure for hardware decoding, for 4K at 60 frames a second or for the cost in VR, and the timings are from a software renderer; HDR files are only shown with a note (no tone mapping, no colour management) and were tested with clips made by the FFmpeg encoder, not with files from cameras; chroma siting is not read, BT.2020 constant luminance is treated as non constant luminance and SMPTE 240M uses the height rule; whether the buffer of the hardware copy is allocated again for each frame could not be checked without a hardware decoder; the new tooltips and the HDR note were compiled and covered by the tests of the status data, but not looked at by hand in a window.

## 0.30.0 - 2026-11-24

- Advanced Output 2.0, masks: three new ways to make a mask, next to the polygon. Smooth edges turns the corners into curves (cubic Bezier, two handles per point, drag the blue squares, Alt moves one handle alone, Straight edges goes back; Add point splits an edge without changing its shape). Freehand lets you draw the shape with the mouse and reduces the stroke to at most 32 points that stay editable. Mask from image uses a picture (PNG, JPG, BMP or TGA up to 16 MiB, copied into the `masks` folder of the data folder; its alpha channel if it has one, otherwise its brightness) stretched over the output. A picture is decoded on a thread of its own and fails closed: while it loads, or when the file is missing, damaged or too large, the slice stays hidden (also with Invert), never shows unmasked, and is retried after five seconds or with Reload image. Invert and Soft edge work for all kinds.
- Colour per slice: each slice has its own gain per channel, saturation, contrast, gamma and black lift (Colour of this slice), applied before the colour of the output inside the same output shader (one more uniform pair, no extra pass or texture), skipped at the default, not applied to the test pattern and not outside a mask. Saved as `sliceColor`.
- Mapped NDI per output: every output tab has Send this output as NDI and a name (default `NyrexVJ Output 1` to `4`). The source carries the picture exactly as the output window shows it (slices, corners, warp, masks, edge blending, colour, delay) in the size of the window, read back from the back buffer through two pixel buffers one frame late, only while a receiver is connected and for windows up to 4096 pixels. The sender restarts on a new name and stops when NDI is turned off or the window closes. Without the NDI runtime the output keeps drawing, the tab says `The NDI runtime is not installed` and NyrexVJ looks again every three seconds. The NDI of the main picture in Settings is unchanged.
- Budget and old files: masks, meshes, the delay ring and the NDI readback share one budget of 384 MiB of video memory, shown as `used / 384 MiB` in the output tab with an orange line when a setup would need more; `estimateOutputBudget` computes it without a graphics card and the self test checks that it equals the measured memory exactly. Only the NDI readback is refused when it does not fit, the output keeps drawing. All new JSON keys (`sliceColor`, `ndi`, a mask's `curves` and `image`) are written only when they differ from the default, so a venue setup or settings file without the new features is byte for byte what 0.29.0 wrote and every older file loads unchanged; damaged values are repaired, and a picture name with a path in it is dropped.
- Tests, measurements and documents: `nvj_output_tests` (25,040 checks, 6 new groups for curves, strokes, the picture decoder and cache, the budget and the new keys), `nvj_venue_tests` (119 checks, old files byte for byte, new keys, damaged keys) and `NyrexVJ --output-selftest` (470 checks with the fake NDI library, 446 without; curved, freehand and picture masks against a CPU model, the order of slice and output colour, NDI against the fake library with name change, budget refusal and the fallback without a runtime); the CI now starts the self test with `NYREXVJ_NDI_LIB` set to the fake library. 44 new texts in all seven translations (`scripts/check_i18n.py` 1,539 keys, `nvj_i18n_tests` 87,712 checks), and all 29 tests, `--check-shaders`, `--live-selftest`, `--plugin-selftest` and `--shader-selftest` pass on Linux. Measured with the Mesa software renderer at 1280 x 720: slice and output colour 10.3 to 12.0 ms per frame (output colour alone 7.5 to 9.2), a curved mask 7.3 to 8.6 ms (a polygon 7.4 to 8.3), NDI of an output with one receiver 7.3 to 9.6 ms against 4.5 to 6.0 ms without NDI, building a curved 32 point mask 7.0 to 7.2 ms and a picture mask 2.2 to 3.1 ms (three runs, spread up to 25 percent). New and changed documents: ADR 0023, `docs/OUTPUTS.md`, `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`, `docs/INTERFACES.md`, `docs/DECISIONS.md`, `docs/ROADMAP.md` and both READMEs. New code: `src/core/mask_images.cpp`, the Bezier, stroke, picture and budget functions in `src/core/output_map.cpp`, the NDI sender and slice colour in `src/render/output_window.cpp` and the new controls in `src/app/ui_outputs.cpp`.
- Known limits: nothing was run on a real graphics card, real projectors, Windows or with the real NDI runtime and a real receiver (the NDI of an output was tested against a fake library that implements only the calls NyrexVJ makes, so the frame layout and the receiver side are not proven), and the timings are from a software renderer; the editor canvas (dragging handles, drawing freehand, typing a picture path) was compiled and covered through the same functions in tests but not clicked through by hand in a window; NDI sends the size of the window, so a window larger than 4096 pixels sends nothing, and there is no audio; a picture mask is stretched over the output and does not follow the picture, has no preview in the interface and cannot be drawn on, and a venue stores only its name, so the pictures have to be copied to another computer; freehand has no undo; projection onto 3D surfaces and LED fixtures belong to other requests and were not touched.

## 0.29.0 - 2026-11-24

- Shader 2.0 for your own shader clips: a line `#pragma nyrexvj alpha` makes a clip transparent (the colour you write is multiplied by the alpha, or `alpha premultiplied` if your shader does that itself, and the layer is composited with its blend mode and opacity over the layers below). Sections in one file turn it into a multipass shader: `buffer a` to `buffer d` (each optionally `scale 0.25` to `1`), `image`, `common` code that is added to every pass, and `channel N source` lines that say what `iChannel0` to `iChannel3` read. Order of work is Buffer A, B, C, D, then Image, once per frame, the same as Shadertoy; a buffer that reads itself or a later buffer sees the previous frame. Buffers are 16 bit floating point, so values above 1 and below 0 survive from frame to frame. Both the NyrexVJ style and the Shadertoy style work.
- Old files are not touched: a shader without a `#pragma nyrexvj` line is compiled byte for byte as before (a test compares a fixed fingerprint of the generated source), keeps its opaque or pass-through alpha, and presets and sessions that use it look the same. A misspelled directive is an error with its line number instead of being ignored, so a typo cannot silently turn a feature off.
- Inputs: each pass has four channels. A channel can read the audio texture (the default for channel 0, as before), a buffer, an image or a video of the Library (`media "Image: name"`, up to four different ones per file) or nothing, with `nearest` or `linear` and `clamp` or `repeat`. New Shadertoy uniforms `iChannelResolution`, `iChannelTime`, a real `iFrame` and a real `iTimeDelta` (Shader 2.0 only; without a pragma line `iFrame` and `iTimeDelta` are derived as before), and the same data in the NyrexVJ style as `uChannel0` to `uChannel3`, `uChannelRes`, `uChannelTime`, `uFrame` and `uDelta`. Scale zooms the image pass only. Not supported (and documented as such): mouse, keyboard, webcam, cubemaps, 3D textures, mipmaps, sound shaders, live inputs and shader parameters as sliders.
- Memory and errors: every layer that shows a multipass shader has its own buffers (two layers, the old deck during a transition and the preview never share), which restart on a trigger, a deck switch, a reload, a short pause in drawing and a jump back of the shader clock, and are freed after 240 frames without drawing. All buffers together stay inside 512 MiB: a buffer that does not fit runs at a smaller scale (down to a quarter), and when even that does not fit the layer stays transparent, the Clip window says why and the other layers keep running. A compile error names the pass (`Buffer B:`), lists every failing pass and uses the line numbers of your file. The Clip window shows the type, the number of buffers and an alpha hint; Settings, Performance has the buffer memory in the memory report and the benchmark JSON.
- Tests, measurements and documents: `nvj_shader_tests` (248 checks) covers the parser with 31 kinds of mistakes, the source of every pass, the fixed fingerprint of old shaders and the budget function; the new `NyrexVJ --shader-selftest [FRAMES]` (40 checks, also in the CI) draws test shaders through the real engine and checks alpha for both styles, feedback over frames, restart, pass order, `iFrame`, `iTimeDelta`, scale, channel options, an image as input, error names and lines, a broken shader next to a working one, a reload, the release of buffers, the budget and a deck switch with a transition. It found one real bug (a retrigger within a quarter of a second did not restart the buffers), which is fixed. 13 new texts in all seven translations (`nvj_i18n_tests` 85,243 checks, `scripts/check_i18n.py` 1,495 keys), `--check-shaders`, `--live-selftest`, `--plugin-selftest` and `--output-selftest` pass, and all 29 tests pass on Linux. Measured with the Mesa software renderer at 1280 x 720: a plain shader 12.3 ms per frame, an alpha shader 11.8 ms, two buffers 27.3 ms and four buffers 49.3 ms (29.5 and 59.0 MB of buffers). New and changed documents: ADR 0022, `docs/SHADERS.md`, `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`, `docs/INTERFACES.md`, `docs/ROADMAP.md` and both READMEs.
- Known limits: nothing was run on a real graphics card, on Windows or with the drivers of NVIDIA, AMD or Intel (the CI builds on Windows but runs no render test), and nothing next to VRChat, so drivers that are stricter about GLSL may need a small fix in a shader; the timings are from a software renderer and show how the cost grows with every pass, not how fast a graphics card is; a video as a channel was not tested (only an image), nor 4K, and the budget was tested with small budgets through a test function, not by filling the video memory; a video that plays in a layer and is also used as a channel would pull the video to two positions; media inputs are premultiplied, and a thumbnail shows the shader without its images and videos; the error messages of the parser that carry a line number are English only; the Shader 2.0 buttons of the Clip window were looked at in the code and in the self test, not clicked through by hand in a window; importing ISF files is a separate request.

## 0.28.0 - 2026-11-21

- DXV videos become HAP copies in the background: Resolume's DXV codec is decoded on the processor, so NyrexVJ can now make a HAP copy of a DXV video, play the copy through the compressed HAP path (no processor decoding, 0.5 or 1 byte per pixel in video memory instead of 4) and keep the DXV original untouched. All four DXV variants are recognised from the texture tag in the first packet: Normal Quality (DXT1) becomes HAP, Normal Quality with alpha (DXT5) becomes HAP Alpha, High Quality (YCG6) becomes HAP Q, and High Quality with alpha (YG10) becomes HAP Alpha. Size, every frame and its time (so also a changing frame rate), the alpha channel and the colours (High Quality is YCoCg, converted with the full range) are kept, and the finished copy is read back and compared with the source before it is used. HAP Q with transparency (HAP Q Alpha) was checked and is **not supported**, neither by NyrexVJ nor by FFmpeg's HAP encoder, so a video with transparency always becomes HAP Alpha; a format picked under Settings, Performance, HAP format is only used when it keeps the picture as it is.
- Import mode, background work and cache: Settings, Performance, Convert DXV videos to HAP has Off, Ask and Automatic (default Ask, because converting uses disk space and processor time that nobody agreed to yet). Ask shows a notice card with the DXV videos it found and converts after your answer (Not now is remembered), Automatic converts every DXV video it finds, and Convert library now looks at the whole library again. Only `.mov` files are looked at. One video at a time is converted on one thread with a lowered priority, with a progress bar and a Cancel button on the cards and in the settings, and a conversion that fails or is cancelled leaves no partial file. The copies are kept in `cache/hap` in the NyrexVJ data folder, named after the size, the beginning and the end of the file and the settings, so a moved or renamed file finds its copy again and a changed file does not. A size limit (default 4 GB, 256 MB to 200 GB) deletes the copies used longest ago first and never one that is playing; Clear cache and Open cache folder are in the settings. Original files are only opened for reading, never changed.
- Playback and fallback: when a clip starts, NyrexVJ asks for a converted copy and plays it through the existing HAP pipeline. While a copy is still being made the DXV original plays, and the copy takes over without a gap as soon as it exists (the new decoder is opened first and swapped in). If the copy cannot be opened or played, it is marked bad, is not offered again and the DXV original plays on. New settings `convertMode`, `convertFormat`, `convertCacheMb` and `ffmpegHintDismissed` are validated when loaded; HAP, H.264 and every other path are unchanged.
- FFmpeg check and setup by button: a silent check on its own thread after the window opened looks for the FFmpeg libraries, a DXV decoder, a HAP encoder that works (all three variants are tried with a small picture), the MOV writer and Snappy, and shows the result in Settings, Performance, in the section FFmpeg. If something is missing, a notice card offers the setup (its button only opens the page). Download and set up FFmpeg downloads a shared LGPL build from the release `latest` of BtbN/FFmpeg-Builds over HTTPS (FFmpeg 8.1 or 9.0, chosen by the library version NyrexVJ was built for), compares its SHA-256 with the `checksums.sha256` of the same release, unpacks it with NyrexVJ's own zip and tar.xz reader (limits, no paths that leave the folder, no links or encryption), accepts it only when `LICENSE.txt` is the LGPL text and the build configuration has neither `--enable-gpl` nor `--enable-nonfree`, and installs it into the `ffmpeg` folder of the NyrexVJ data folder through a staging folder. Nothing is downloaded before you press the button; an FFmpeg anywhere else on the computer is never touched, an existing NyrexVJ `ffmpeg` folder is kept as `ffmpeg.previous`; the page shows each step with a progress bar, Cancel and errors in your language and has Set up by hand as the alternative. Windows and Linux, about 700 MB of free space while it works. The checksum comes from the same release as the file, so it protects against damaged and wrong files but not against a compromised release; `docs/FFMPEG.md` says so.
- Measurements: `NyrexVJ --bench-convert RUNS:file1,file2` (also `--convert-format` and `--convert-keep DIR`, no window) measures conversion time, decode and encode time, frames per second, speed against playback, size and processor time, and `scripts/bench_video.py --dxv FILE` plays a DXV file and its HAP copy through `--bench-video`. ## 0.26.0 - 2026-11-21

- Plugins: a plugin is a shared library (`.dll` or `.so`) with a small C interface that adds sources to NyrexVJ. Two kinds exist: modulation sources (a number from 0 to 1, about 60 times a second) and image sources (1280 x 720, RGBA, about 30 frames a second). **A plugin is native code, runs with the same rights as NyrexVJ and there is no sandbox**; plugins are off until you tick them in Settings, Plugins, and `--no-plugins` switches them off for a run. See `docs/PLUGINS.md` and ADR 0018.
- The interface and the manifest: `plugins/include/nyrexvj_plugin.h` is a C header, API 1.0, with size tagged structs (fields can be appended in a minor version), one entry function and a host table with log, audio features, beat and parameters. Each plugin sits in a folder with a `plugin.json` (format `nyrexvj-plugin`, version 1). The manifest and the API version are checked before any plugin code runs: the major number must be the same, the minor number at most the program's, otherwise the plugin is listed as not compatible with the reason and never loaded. The header is 1.0 and **not yet declared stable**; that happens after one release of use.
- Isolation: plugin code never runs on the render thread. Loading runs on a loader thread, and each running source has its own thread. Every call has a time budget (modulation 20 ms, image 100 ms, create and destroy 500 ms, loading 1 s). A call that is over its budget is abandoned and the plugin is switched off for the session; an error, a wrong source table, a changed image description or a write behind the image buffer (64 guard bytes are checked) does the same. Libraries are never unloaded, so an abandoned thread can not run into freed code. A modulation value that is not a number becomes 0 and the log of a plugin is limited to 20 lines a second.
- Crashes and safe mode: before each call into a plugin its id is written to a small table that the crash handler copies into the crash report as `plugin: <id>`. ## 0.24.0 - 2026-11-21

- One beat clock: everything that follows the beat (effects, clip length in beats, modulation, OSC output, the BPM display of the deck) now reads one clock that several sources can feed: tapping, the audio analysis, MIDI clock, timecode and Pro DJ Link beats. Automatic takes the best source that is present (Pro DJ Link, timecode, MIDI clock, audio, manual) and falls back when it is lost; a fixed choice stays on its source. - Timecode: LTC is decoded from an audio input of its own (24, 25, 29.97 drop frame and 30 frames a second, noise, DC offset, inverted signal, dropouts and speed changes are handled), and MTC is decoded from the MIDI input (quarter frames and full frame messages, with the 1.75 frame correction). The timecode sets the position of the beat clock (`beat = seconds x BPM / 60`), LTC wins over MTC, and the page shows the time, the frame rate, whether it is running and the counts of good and rejected frames. A signal that is lost for half a second is dropped.
- Cues and modulation: a cue list (up to 512 cues, saved as `cues.json`) fires any control target at a timecode time, once each, and skips cues after a jump or a step of more than half a second so a seek does not fire a wave of cues. The Sync page edits the list with a target picker. Timecode second and Timecode minute are new modulation sources.
- MIDI clock: ticks, Start, Continue, Stop and song position are followed. Ticks are time stamped in the MIDI input thread, so the frame rate adds no jitter. The tempo comes from a short window of tick intervals; a Start makes the position known; without a Start only the tempo is used. Measured against a simulator the phase error stays at 0.006 beat.
- Pro DJ Link: a passive listener for the beat packets of Pioneer players and mixers on UDP port 50001, opened shared so other programs can listen at the same time, off by default. It never announces itself and sends nothing, takes tempo, beat in the bar and phase, and shows the players heard and the packet counts (ignored and malformed ones included). Measured against a packet simulator the phase offset is 0.04 beat (beats are stamped when they are read, so the accuracy is one frame). No play state and no master flag are used.
- StageLinq (Denon): evaluated and not built. The protocol is reverse engineered, a device must be announced and reached over several TCP services, and nothing could be tested, so a safe passive subset is not possible. The reasons and what works today (MIDI clock from the mixer, the audio analysis) are in the document. A future source only has to report observations to the beat clock.
- Output frame lock: with several outputs open, all windows are drawn first and then swapped right after each other. The time between the first and the last swap of two 1280 x 720 windows fell from 4.5 to 5.1 ms to 0.34 to 0.35 ms (software OpenGL). Every output has a Delay of 0 to 8 frames for slower projectors; the delay ring is shared, exists only while a delay is set and takes 23.7 MiB and 0.6 ms a frame at 1080p with a delay of 2. The swap skew is measured and shown over 120 frames, and the new Sync bars pattern (binary frame counter, flash, moving bar) shows with one camera how many frames apart outputs are. This is not a hardware genlock.
- New files, tests and texts: `docs/SYNC.md`, ADR 0016, a section in `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`, `src/core/beat_clock.*`, `timecode.*`, `ltc.*`, `midi_clock.*`, `djlink.*`, `cue_list.*`, `frame_lock.*`, `src/control/sync_system.*`, `djlink_input.*`, `src/render/texture_history.*`, `src/app/ui_sync.cpp` and six new test programs (99 checks for the clock, 364 for timecode, 28,185 for Pro DJ Link, 246 for MIDI clock, 48 for the sync system, 130 for the frame lock) plus 29 new checks in `--output-selftest` (93 in all); about 91 new texts in German, Spanish and French.
