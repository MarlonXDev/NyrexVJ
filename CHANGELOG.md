# Changelog

## 0.68.0 - 2026-11-28
- Music on 3D materials (new), after Marlon asked for the music to move the material of a 3D scene, not only its size and light: the bindings of an object in the Clip Creator can now drive its colour, roughness, texture and surface. Nothing is analysed again; the new sources are made from numbers the program already had, the preview repeats exactly, and clips and presets keep the rows.
- Sources and targets (new): **Beat** (a pulse that follows the beat clock, so it works with tap tempo and without sound) and **Band 1 to 8** (eight slices of the spectrum, about 30 Hz to 16 kHz) join Bass, Mid, High, Level and Kick. On an object the targets **Colour shift** (turns the hue, 1.00 is one whole turn), **Roughness**, **Texture shift X** and **Y**, **Inflate** (pushes the surface out along its normal) and **Ripple** (a wave over the surface) join Size, Glow, Twist and Lift. Rows on the same target add up and a child object takes what its parent got.
- Four rows (changed): an object, a light and the camera have four audio rows instead of two. A scene that uses only the old sources, the old targets and two rows is saved exactly as before and opens in older programs; one that uses more is saved as scene version 2 and a program older than 0.68.0 refuses it with its message about a newer program, instead of playing it wrong.
- Unknown values (changed): a row with a source or a target the program does not know, a row that is not an object and a row after the fourth used to become a silent Bass to Size or were dropped without a word. They are now left out and named in the warnings under the Clip Creator preview and in the log (at most 16 lines); the scene still plays. A light or camera row with a target that does not fit is changed to the one that does, as before, and is now named too.
- The switch and the texts (new): **Settings, Performance, Music moves 3D materials**, on at the start, written to `settings.json` only when it is off. Off leaves the six material targets out and draws the material as authored; Size, Glow, Twist, Lift, Light and Zoom keep following the music. 8 new texts in all seven tables.
- Documents (new): [ADR 0062](docs/adr/0062-audio-reactive-3d-materials-sources-and-targets-for-scene-bindings.md) with the design, the numbers, what was not built and one open question, [SCENE3D.md](docs/SCENE3D.md), [PERFORMANCE.md](docs/PERFORMANCE.md), the manual in English and German (Objects, materials and animation), INTERFACES.md (scene version 2, the settings key) and the test matrix (new area `scene-materials` with `M-MBD-1`).
- **Not built, on purpose:** recomputing the normals of a deformed mesh (a deep ripple on a sharp shape is lit as if it were flat), metallic, opacity and an RGB tint as targets, colour shift, roughness and texture shift in the classic shading of the built-in scenes, and a texture that keeps scrolling with the music. The last one needs a value that is kept between frames, so the preview would no longer repeat from one moment alone; it is an open question in the ADR.
- Tests, measurements and known limits: `nvj_matbind_tests` is new (867 checks: the beat pulse, the band means, every source and target with its limits, the switch, inheritance, repeating exactly, the document keys and their round trip, version 1 for old scenes and 2 for new ones, unknown keys and their notes, a clip pack round trip and the setting); `NyrexVJ --scene-selftest` has 22 new checks through a real OpenGL context and passes 46 of 46 (a third of a colour turn moves a red sphere to green and a whole turn leaves 0 different pixels, Roughness changes the shine, a texture shift moves the texture and a whole shift comes back, Inflate makes the sphere cover 51 % more pixels and a negative amount 40 % fewer, Ripple moves with the time and repeats exactly, the switch off equals a scene without bindings while Size still follows the music, two players give the same bytes in 12 music moments, a document with an unknown source and target plays with two notes); a fault put into the shader for hue, texture shift, Inflate and Ripple and the switch made 1, 2, 4 and 2 of them fail. A check found a real fault on the way: a value that is not a number from the analysis became a NaN in a matrix, so the sources and each sum are now cleaned. Cost on this machine (Mesa software OpenGL): the evaluation of 512 instances takes 13 to 16 microseconds more per frame with four material bindings on every object (70.7 to 77.7 against 83.5 to 87.1), the frame time of 24 spheres is inside the noise (6.0 to 6.3 ms without, 5.6 to 7.9 ms with), the graphics memory does not change. One test fixed on the way: the network sync self test checked the clip names on the follower right after a deck switch without waiting, and failed once in four runs under load, then passed three times alone; it now waits for them like it waits for the deck. **Not checked:** a real graphics card, Windows at run time, a long show, and the combo lists of the editor operated by hand (the editor was drawn and its screenshot looked at, but no click went through it); see `M-MBD-1`.

## 0.67.0 - 2026-11-28
- GPU effect graph optimizer (new), after Marlon asked for a cheaper effect graph: when the master chain is empty and the colour grade, the level, Blackout and the hold change nothing, the last pass of a deck only copied its input. The last layer is now drawn straight into the picture that pass would have written, so one full pass over the picture is gone in every frame. The picture is the same, byte for byte.
- Colour look (changed): with a LUT the look used to render into a scratch picture and copy it back. It now reads the finished picture and writes the output, so the scratch picture is never made and the graphics memory of the look (Settings, Colour, Extra graphics memory) is smaller by one picture of the output size, 3.7 MB at 1280 x 720 and 8.3 MB at 1920 x 1080.
- Transitions (changed): while a deck transition runs, the outgoing and the incoming deck are drawn straight into the two pictures the transition reads when they have nothing to grade, so the two final passes go. Switching the setting in the middle of a transition is safe.
- The switch (new): **Settings, Performance, Render graph, Skip passes that only copy**, on at the start, with a line that counts the passes and copies skipped. It is written to `settings.json` only when it is off, so every older file is written as before. A master effect, a grade, a level below full, Blackout or the hold keep the old path, because they change the picture.
- Translations: 5 new texts in all seven tables.
- Documents (new): [ADR 0061](docs/adr/0061-gpu-effect-graph-optimizer-skip-passes-that-only-copy.md) with the design, the numbers and what was not built, [PERFORMANCE.md](docs/PERFORMANCE.md), the manual in English and German (Passes that only copy), INTERFACES.md (the settings key) and the test matrix (new area `graph-optimizer` with `M-GRF-1`).
- **Not built, on purpose:** fusing effects into one shader (it changes the rounding between effects), removing the copy of feedback effects (the 0.66.0 tests cannot see that go wrong), not drawing covered layers (the alpha of a clip is not known on the processor), skipping the final pass behind a master effect (an effect can leave an alpha below 1) and throttling the previews.
- Tests, measurements and known limits: `nvj_graph_tests` is new (224 checks: the setting and its limits, when the last pass is neutral, when a chain is empty, the route table, the swap parity from 0 to 64 and the counters); `NyrexVJ --shader-selftest` has 73 new render checks and passes 263 of 263 (every one draws the same frame with the switch off and on and compares every byte: one to four layers, all 14 blend modes, five blend orders, six grades, three levels, Blackout, hold, master and layer effects, the colour look in six variants, four kinds of transition (Fade, Radial, Glitch, custom), four feedback scenes over 20 frames and a switch in the middle of a transition); a deliberate fault in the neutral test made 9 of them fail. The colour test (`--color-selftest`) passes 37 of 37 after its memory check was adapted. Frame time on software OpenGL at 1280 x 720, switch off to on, average: one layer 17.1 to 10.7 ms (-37.5 %), four layers 50.6 to 44.5 ms (-12.1 %), four layers with a colour look 65.2 to 56.7 ms (-13.0 %) and 3.7 MB less memory, four layers with a master effect 57.1 to 56.9 ms (no change, as intended), a fade between two decks 85.5 to 71.2 ms (-16.7 %), 0 different pixels in all five. One thing found by the build service on the first push: the GPU diagnostics self test waited for a released image to load again by counting 1,200 frames, which only worked because even an empty frame cost time; without the extra pass the 1,200 frames took 11 ms, less than the loader needs (254 ms here). The program was right, the test was wrong; it now also waits by the clock (up to 15 seconds). **Not checked:** a real graphics card (a pass costs 6 to 7 ms here because a processor draws every pixel; on a card the saving in time will be much smaller, the saving in passes and memory stays), Windows at run time (built in CI only), two PCs on the network sync and a run of hours; see `M-GRF-1`.

## 0.66.0 - 2026-11-28
- GPU feedback lab (new), after Marlon asked for safer feedback effects: the pictures that feedback effects keep now have a **memory budget** (Settings, Performance, Feedback; 256 MB at the start, 0 turns it off, at least 64 MB). When it is used up, a new buffer is made three quarters, half or a quarter of the picture and the effect still works; if not even that fits, the effect shows the live picture without trails and the page counts the refusals. The existing effects Trails and Hue Feedback use it too, and their shaders are not changed. No second engine: the buffers are the same ones as before, in the same place.
- Four new effects in the Library under Effects, each with four sliders: **Time Echo** (a fading copy, optionally pushed away and blurred), **Motion Smear** (a slow smoothing over time, with a slide), **Feedback Transform** (a tunnel with zoom, rotation and drift, the kick adds a small zoom) and **Accumulate** (average, keep the brightest, or a long exposure that builds up, with gain and a kick flush). Their fade is set as a half-life in real seconds, so a trail lasts the same at 30, 60 or 144 frames per second and holds still while the show is paused; they round at random so a slow fade really reaches black. The shader header has a new `uDelta` (the time between two frames) and helpers `fbKeep`, `fbFetch` and `fbRound` for effects of your own.
- Freeze and Reset (new): the effect rack shows **Freeze feedback** and **Reset feedback** when a chain has a feedback effect. Freeze holds every feedback picture while the live picture is still drawn over it (not saved with the show); Reset clears them in the same frame and restarts the buffers of shaders. Both are control targets (`master/feedback-freeze`, `master/feedback-reset`), so MIDI, OSC, the web interface and the timeline can use them. **The network sync schema changes with the two new ids: a master and a follower must both be on 0.66.0.**
- Reuse and memory (new): a released buffer waits up to five seconds in a pool of at most eight and is used again instead of asking the graphics card for new memory (Reuse feedback buffers, on at the start). The memory overview has a new line Feedback buffers, and feedback buffers are no longer counted as render targets. The benchmark report has `feedbackBytes`. The two new settings (`feedbackBudgetMb`, `feedbackReuse`) are written only when they are not the default, so the settings file of everyone who never touches them is written as before.
- Translations: 18 new texts in all seven tables.
- Documents (new): [ADR 0060](docs/adr/0060-gpu-feedback-lab-budget-pool-freeze-reset-and-feedback-effects.md) with the design, the numbers and the open question, [PERFORMANCE.md](docs/PERFORMANCE.md), the manual in English and German (Feedback effects), INTERFACES.md (the two control ids, the two settings keys, the network sync note) and the test matrix (new area `feedback-lab` with `M-FBK-1`).
- **Not built, on purpose:** a change to Trails and Hue Feedback (an open question in the ADR: they decay per frame, so a trail is about four times shorter at 144 than at 60 frames per second, and at the top of their slider Trails never gets below a grey of 33 of 255), feedback pictures with more than 8 bit, Freeze for the buffers of shader clips and filters, and a budget per layer or in milliseconds.
- Tests, measurements and known limits: `nvj_feedback_tests` is new (3,740 checks: the settings and their limits, the two control targets, the four effects and the unchanged text of Trails and Hue Feedback, the budget arithmetic, a graph with feedback, a chain at the node limit and a graph forced into a loop); `NyrexVJ --shader-selftest` has 48 new render checks and passes 190 of 190 (the half-life at 30, 60 and 120 frames per second and across a pause, Freeze, Reset, the modes of Accumulate, the budget with smaller and refused buffers, eight resolution changes, reuse, and a 1,200 frame run with random changes that peaked at 12.4 MB and ended at 0 bytes with no GL error); `ctest` 66 of 66, `--check-shaders` exit 0, `--interface-uitest` 116 of 116 on a virtual screen. The frozen settings and preset files of `nvj_compat_tests` did not change. Two things found on the way: a smooth reduction of a smaller buffer rounded to the nearest colour step and left a grey of 42 of 255 for ever (now filled by taking every n-th pixel), and a smaller buffer still fades more slowly in the last steps than a full size one (8 % of the pixels one step above black after 15 seconds against 0 %). Frame time on software OpenGL at 1280 x 720: no effect 14.4 ms, Trails 23.5, Time Echo 31.5, Motion Smear 26.8, Feedback Transform 26.3, Accumulate 25.8; four layers 43.0 ms without and 110.4 ms with a Time Echo on each. **Not checked:** a real graphics card, Windows at run time (built in CI only), two PCs on the network sync, a run of hours, MIDI and OSC hardware for Freeze and Reset, and a look next to the old effects on a real output; see `M-FBK-1`.

## 0.64.0 - 2026-11-27
- Improved beat tracker (new, optional), after Marlon asked for a better audio analysis and sync: **Settings, Audio, Beat tracking** now has the choice **Classic** (the detector that was always there, still the default) and **Improved**. Improved finds the tempo from all pairs of onsets of the last ten seconds, so one missing kick does not break it, shows how sure it is about the tempo and about the beat, and says when it is locked. While it is locked the beats come on a steady grid one period apart that goes on through a short break and stops 1.5 s after the sound has gone; the tempo is kept for ten seconds. The onsets are found from the rise of the spectrum in three bands and stamped with the moment the rise crossed a threshold, inside the frame. The beat clock, OSC `/nyrex/out/onset`, MIDI clock, LTC, MTC and Pro DJ Link work as before and follow the grid like they followed the detections.
- Audio latency (new): a slider from -250 to +250 ms moves the grid beats earlier (positive) or later (negative); because the grid predicts, a beat can be placed before the sound has been analysed. It is only used with Improved and is separate from the Sync latency of the clock.
- Band split (new): **Bass and mid split** (60 to 400 Hz) and **Mid and high split** (800 to 6000 Hz) move the borders of the three bands for both methods. The defaults, 150 Hz and 2000 Hz, are the old values.
- Channel choice (new): **Channel** takes Mix (default), Left or Right from a device with two channels. On Linux the capture only asks PulseAudio for two channels when Left or Right is chosen, so Mix is the same capture as before; on Windows the first or second channel is picked while the frames are converted. The box is grey for the demo signal and for inputs that cannot do it. A change restarts the capture of the device.
- Translations: 10 new texts in all seven tables (six labels, three tooltips and one note).
- Documents (new): [ADR 0058](docs/adr/0058-audio-analysis-beat-tracker-latency-bands-and-channels.md) with the design, the measured numbers and what was not done, [SYNC.md](docs/SYNC.md) (a section Audio analysis 2 and the untested list), the manual in English and German (Analysis settings), the settings keys in INTERFACES.md, the test matrix (new area `audio-analysis` with `M-AUD-1`) and new pictures of the Audio pages.
- **Not built, on purpose:** a second audio engine or a second analysis input, ASIO or exclusive mode, an automatic latency measurement (it needs a loopback cable and a known signal), recognising which song is playing, new OSC messages, per-band onset events, tempi below 80 or above 180 BPM, and making Improved the default.
- Tests, measurements and known limits: `nvj_audio_tracker_tests` is new (7029 checks on synthetic tracks: three tempi, a tempo change from 120 to 140 BPM, a cross-fade between 124 and 128 BPM, silence and its return, a quiet break, clipping, hats on the off-beats, three frame rates, the latency in both directions, the channel picking, the band split, the CPU time, and that the classic path is unchanged by switching). Classic against Improved on the same tracks: the tempo is within 2 percent after 2.97 s against 1.27 s at 128 BPM, a change from 120 to 140 BPM is followed after 5.22 s against 2.42 s, and the median tempo error at 128 BPM is 0.57 against 0.05 BPM; after silence the grid is back within 0.30 s; the extra CPU time per frame is about 4 us on average. `nvj_compat_tests` has a new frozen settings file `settings_v0.64.0.json` (the writer gained five keys) and loads `settings_v0.50.0.json` as a file of an earlier release with the new keys at their defaults; `--check-shaders` exit 0, `--interface-uitest` 116 of 116 passed on a virtual screen, `ctest` 64 of 64. **Not checked:** real music and real DJ mixes, real audio devices and a loopback measurement of the latency, the two-channel capture on a real PulseAudio server, the Windows capture at run time (built in CI only), other frame rates and other machines for the CPU numbers; the offset of the detector to the sound depends on the frame rate and has to be covered with the latency setting; see `M-AUD-1`.

## 0.58.0 - 2026-11-26
- Ping and answer (new): a program can send `/nyrex/ping` with one whole number from 0 to 16777215 to the OSC input and NyrexVJ answers `/nyrex/out/pong` with the same number on the OSC output, in the frame that reads it. The program that sent the ping measures the time between the two. This is a new address inside OSC schema 1, so the schema number stays 1 and receivers that do not know it ignore it. The answer goes to the address in the OSC output settings, never to the sender of the ping, and exists only while OSC input and OSC output are both on. At most 8 pings are taken per frame, a wrong type, a number out of range or more than one argument is refused, and answers still waiting are dropped when the output restarts.

## 0.48.0 - 2026-11-25
- Network sync (new, **off by default**): Settings, Sync, Network sync lets several PCs that run NyrexVJ follow one master. The master sends its beat, tempo, play and pause, timecode, the levels, colours, effects, macros and blackout of the show, the active deck and the clip that plays on each layer; a follower takes them over. Choose the role Master on one PC and Follower on the others, copy the key from the master to every follower, type the IPv4 address of the master and the same port on both sides. - Protocol and safety (new): UDP with a version, a hash of the list of controls (the schema), a growing sequence number, a full state and small updates, a check that a gap was filled, and a signature (HMAC-SHA256 with a key of 256 bits that is made on the master and kept in `netsync_key` in the data folder) on every packet. A packet with a wrong signature is counted and never answered. It is signed and **not encrypted**: use it in a network you trust. Different program versions agree on a protocol; if the controls differ the follower follows only the beat (Clock only) and says so, and a protocol that cannot be spoken is refused with a reason. A lost connection is noticed after 3 s, the follower keeps the last values and the tempo, stays paused for its timeline and cues and reconnects on its own; after that only what changed on the master is applied. A master serves 8 followers at most.
- Media is never sent. The master sends the names of its decks and clips; a follower shows which clip is missing here, which is another clip here and which deck is not in use, and does not start a clip whose name differs. The clock is estimated with a ping once a second from the best round trips, with drift tracked only when it is significant, and the beat of a follower is predicted from it. - Shader 2.0 for your own shader clips: a line `#pragma nyrexvj alpha` makes a clip transparent (the colour you write is multiplied by the alpha, or `alpha premultiplied` if your shader does that itself, and the layer is composited with its blend mode and opacity over the layers below). See `docs/PLUGINS.md` and ADR 0018.
- The interface and the manifest: `plugins/include/nyrexvj_plugin.h` is a C header, API 1.0, with size tagged structs (fields can be appended in a minor version), one entry function and a host table with log, audio features, beat and parameters. Each plugin sits in a folder with a `plugin.json` (format `nyrexvj-plugin`, version 1). The manifest and the API version are checked before any plugin code runs: the major number must be the same, the minor number at most the program's, otherwise the plugin is listed as not compatible with the reason and never loaded. The header is 1.0 and **not yet declared stable**; that happens after one release of use.
- Isolation: plugin code never runs on the render thread. Loading runs on a loader thread, and each running source has its own thread. Every call has a time budget (modulation 20 ms, image 100 ms, create and destroy 500 ms, loading 1 s). A call that is over its budget is abandoned and the plugin is switched off for the session; an error, a wrong source table, a changed image description or a write behind the image buffer (64 guard bytes are checked) does the same. Libraries are never unloaded, so an abandoned thread can not run into freed code. A modulation value that is not a number becomes 0 and the log of a plugin is limited to 20 lines a second.
- Crashes and safe mode: before each call into a plugin its id is written to a small table that the crash handler copies into the crash report as `plugin: <id>`. At the next start the plugin is put into the list `plugins/disabled.json` before anything is loaded and shows as Safe mode; it comes back only when you press Enable again. A crash in a plugin still ends the program once, because the plugin runs inside the program (a separate process is a documented next step).
- In the program: Settings, Plugins lists the plugins with their state, a warning, the sources, a value bar and the slowest call of each modulation source and up to four parameter sliders for each source. Eight modulation slots appear as the bands `Plugin 1` to `Plugin 8` in every list of sources (modulations, effect reactions) once they are assigned, and four image slots give the clips `Plugin Source 1` to `Plugin Source 4`, which behave like a camera or an NDI input. A modulation source stops 3 seconds after the last read and an image source runs while its clip is on a layer. Plugin bands are not available in the stage, whose list of sources is fixed.
- An example and a check: `plugins/example` is a plugin in C99 without a dependency, with a modulation source (pulse) and an image source (plasma), built by CMake into `build/plugins/nyrexvj-example`. `--plugin-selftest FOLDER` loads a plugin through the real engine and checks the modulation range and variation, the image size, content and upload, the call times and that no thread is left behind (`--bench-out` writes JSON). CI runs it on Linux.
- Tests and numbers: `nvj_plugin_tests` (294 checks, about 10 seconds) builds the example plugin and 20 broken variants of a test plugin and covers the wrong major version and a newer minor version (never loaded), a slow, hanging, failing, crashing and out of range plugin, a slow load, damaged tables, a write behind the image and a log that never stops, a crash in a child process that leads to safe mode and back, and a render thread that stays responsive while a plugin hangs or loads slowly (the longest call stayed below 100 ms). The tests also run under the address and undefined behaviour sanitizers. The render thread spent 0.05 to 0.08 microseconds a frame with plugins off or nothing enabled and 0.16 to 0.21 with the example plugin running in two slots (a throwaway program, five runs, Linux, release build). The example image source delivered 30.5 frames a second.
- New files, tests and texts: `docs/PLUGINS.md`, ADR 0018, a section in `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`, rows in `docs/INTERFACES.md` and `docs/DEPENDENCIES.md`, `plugins/include/nyrexvj_plugin.h`, `plugins/example`, `tests/plugins/broken_plugin.c`, `tests/test_plugins.cpp`, `src/core/plugin_manifest.*`, `plugin_config.*`, `plugin_host.*`, `dynlib.*`, `src/app/ui_plugins.cpp` and 34 new texts in German, Spanish and French. No new dependency: libraries are opened with `dlopen` and `LoadLibrary`.
- Known limits: the plugin tests pass on Windows in CI (`.dll` loading, crash handler), but no plugin was used by hand on Windows and `--plugin-selftest` runs only on Linux; nothing was tried with a plugin written in another language than C, with a plugin that does real work, next to VRChat or on a real graphics card. Plugins cannot draw with OpenGL, cannot be replaced while the program runs, give only one picture size (1280 x 720) and are not isolated in a process; a plugin that writes into memory that is not its own can still crash the program.

## 0.24.0 - 2026-11-21

- One beat clock: everything that follows the beat (effects, clip length in beats, modulation, OSC output, the BPM display of the deck) now reads one clock that several sources can feed: tapping, the audio analysis, MIDI clock, timecode and Pro DJ Link beats. Automatic takes the best source that is present (Pro DJ Link, timecode, MIDI clock, audio, manual) and falls back when it is lost; a fixed choice stays on its source. Phase errors are removed gradually (at most a quarter of the error per observation) so a picture on the beat does not stutter, a jump of more than a beat is followed at once, and a latency offset of -500 to +500 ms moves the beat. The state (Free running, Searching, Acquiring, Locked, Lost), the phase error and the source are shown in the new Settings, Sync page. The old Sync BPM to audio setting works as before.
- Timecode: LTC is decoded from an audio input of its own (24, 25, 29.97 drop frame and 30 frames a second, noise, DC offset, inverted signal, dropouts and speed changes are handled), and MTC is decoded from the MIDI input (quarter frames and full frame messages, with the 1.75 frame correction). The timecode sets the position of the beat clock (`beat = seconds x BPM / 60`), LTC wins over MTC, and the page shows the time, the frame rate, whether it is running and the counts of good and rejected frames. A signal that is lost for half a second is dropped.
- Cues and modulation: a cue list (up to 512 cues, saved as `cues.json`) fires any control target at a timecode time, once each, and skips cues after a jump or a step of more than half a second so a seek does not fire a wave of cues. The Sync page edits the list with a target picker. Timecode second and Timecode minute are new modulation sources.
- MIDI clock: ticks, Start, Continue, Stop and song position are followed. Ticks are time stamped in the MIDI input thread, so the frame rate adds no jitter. The tempo comes from a short window of tick intervals; a Start makes the position known; without a Start only the tempo is used. Measured against a simulator the phase error stays at 0.006 beat.
- Pro DJ Link: a passive listener for the beat packets of Pioneer players and mixers on UDP port 50001, opened shared so other programs can listen at the same time, off by default. It never announces itself and sends nothing, takes tempo, beat in the bar and phase, and shows the players heard and the packet counts (ignored and malformed ones included). Measured against a packet simulator the phase offset is 0.04 beat (beats are stamped when they are read, so the accuracy is one frame). No play state and no master flag are used.
- StageLinq (Denon): evaluated and not built. The protocol is reverse engineered, a device must be announced and reached over several TCP services, and nothing could be tested, so a safe passive subset is not possible. The reasons and what works today (MIDI clock from the mixer, the audio analysis) are in the document. A future source only has to report observations to the beat clock.
- Output frame lock: with several outputs open, all windows are drawn first and then swapped right after each other. The time between the first and the last swap of two 1280 x 720 windows fell from 4.5 to 5.1 ms to 0.34 to 0.35 ms (software OpenGL). Every output has a Delay of 0 to 8 frames for slower projectors; the delay ring is shared, exists only while a delay is set and takes 23.7 MiB and 0.6 ms a frame at 1080p with a delay of 2. The swap skew is measured and shown over 120 frames, and the new Sync bars pattern (binary frame counter, flash, moving bar) shows with one camera how many frames apart outputs are. This is not a hardware genlock.
- New files, tests and texts: `docs/SYNC.md`, ADR 0016, a section in `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`, `src/core/beat_clock.*`, `timecode.*`, `ltc.*`, `midi_clock.*`, `djlink.*`, `cue_list.*`, `frame_lock.*`, `src/control/sync_system.*`, `djlink_input.*`, `src/render/texture_history.*`, `src/app/ui_sync.cpp` and six new test programs (99 checks for the clock, 364 for timecode, 28,185 for Pro DJ Link, 246 for MIDI clock, 48 for the sync system, 130 for the frame lock) plus 29 new checks in `--output-selftest` (93 in all); about 91 new texts in German, Spanish and French. The cost of the sync system without any source is 0.04 microseconds a frame, decoding LTC takes 0.25 ms for each second of audio.
- Known limits: nothing was tried with real Pioneer or Denon gear, timecode generators, audio interfaces, real MIDI clock hardware, projectors, a real graphics card, several graphics cards, next to VRChat or on Windows at run time. 25 fps at 4 percent slower cannot be told from 24 fps, 29.97 non-drop shows as 30, reverse timecode is not supported, a vanished audio device is not reopened by itself, and the Pro DJ Link time is accurate to one frame. All numbers come from generated signals, a packet simulator and a software rasteriser.

- Video monitor: Settings, Performance, Videos shows for every playing video the decoder and the hardware interface, the route to the graphics card, the reason for a fallback, decode time, upload time, dropped and late pictures and the video memory, split into pictures, colour planes, HAP textures and upload buffers. - Fixed: a looping video lost a few pictures at every restart. New command line options measure the cost of videos (--bench-video) and check the graphics card path against the processor path (--video-selftest), see docs/MEDIA.md and docs/adr/0010-video-decode-pipeline.md.
