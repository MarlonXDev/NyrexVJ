# ADR 0040: Video routing of layers, groups and the master as sources, built into the existing render flow

- **Status:** Accepted for the local part (requested by MarlonXDev as prompt 22). Issue [#108](https://github.com/MarlonXDev/NyrexVJ/issues/108). A free routing graph, layer groups of your own choice, routing per output window and external video protocols (prompt 29) are **not built**.
- **Date:** 2026-11-25
- **Owners:** Maintainers (pending assignment)
- **Related:** [OUTPUTS.md](../OUTPUTS.md), [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md), [INTERFACES.md](../INTERFACES.md), [PERFORMANCE.md](../PERFORMANCE.md), [ADR 0011](0011-render-pipeline-optimisation.md), [ADR 0015](0015-advanced-output.md), [ADR 0038](0038-gpu-diagnostics.md)

## Context
A layer could play a clip, a media file, a live source or a Spout sender, but the picture of one layer could not be used by another, and the three senders (Spout, NDI, the recorder) always sent the finished master. A show that wants to feed a layer back into itself, to send only the background layer to OBS, or to use the mix of the first two layers as the input of a third, had no way to do it. The task asked for a directed routing graph for layers, groups, the master and internal virtual sources, with a preview and an unambiguous choice of source, no cycles except controlled feedback, checks of format, alpha and size, GPU textures without a read back to the processor, rules for lifetime, reference counting, resizing, cache and latency, and tests for a change during playback, several users of one source, a missing source and performance. It said to build only what does not exist.

## Findings (what existed before)
- **Existed:** `Engine::renderDeck` draws the four layers in order into `layerA_`/`layerB_` (or the reduced `layerLo*` pair), runs each layer's effect chain, composites into `compA_`/`compB_` with the blend modes, runs the master chain and `finalPass` into `out_`; two passes during a transition (the outgoing deck draws from copies of its layers); feedback effects with their own double buffers per chain and deck; hidden layers (opacity 0 and no feedback effect) are skipped; `drawLayerSource` as the one place where a layer's clip is drawn (shader, scene, media, generator, live, Spout); `outputTexture()` handed to Spout, NDI, the recorder and the output windows; `GpuMemory` and the diagnostics of v0.45.0; render targets `RenderTarget` (RGBA8, or RGBA16F for float chains).
- **Existed, not enough:** no way to use a layer's picture as a source; senders had no choice; the hidden-layer skip would have made a faded out source layer black.
- **Not found:** a routing graph, virtual sources, a reference count for pictures, a rule for cycles.
- **Not verified:** the behaviour on real graphics cards (the cost of `glBlitFramebuffer` between targets, the cost of an extra full-size copy at 4K), and Windows beyond the CI build.

## Decision
1. **Routing is a kind of clip, not a new graph editor.** `ClipKind::RouteSource` (13 clips in the library under the filter Routing). A layer that plays one is an ordinary layer: its own effect chain, opacity, blend mode and grade apply to the routed picture. This keeps every existing control, save format and OSC address unchanged, and the choice of source is unambiguous because it is the name of the clip in a cell. The 13 clips are the same-frame sources Layer 1 to 3 and Layers 1-2 and 1-3, and the feedback sources (previous frame) Layer 1 to 4, Layers 1-2, 1-3, 1-4 and Master.
2. **What a source is.** Layer N is the picture of layer N after its effects and before its opacity, blend mode and colour grade. Layers 1-n is the mix of the first n layers after their opacity, blend modes and grades, before the master effects. Master is the picture after the master effects and before the master level, grade and blackout. A source that is not playing (empty cell, bypass, solo of another layer) is **inactive**.
3. **A pure core.** `core/routing.*` (in `nvj_core`, no OpenGL) holds the sources and their names and slots, the rules (`classify`, `makePlan`), the check of surfaces (`checkSurfaces`: empty, over 8192 on a side, format change, scaling, flattening of alpha) and the cache tracker. It is tested by `nvj_routing_tests` (34,049 checks, exhaustive over all combinations of layers and sources, random consumer lists with an acyclic check, a 600 step switching simulation).
4. **No cycles except one frame of feedback.** A same-frame source must be drawn before its user in the same frame: Layer k for a layer below it, Layers 1-n for layer n or higher. Everything else (a layer that uses itself, a layer below that uses a layer above, a layer that uses the master of the same frame) is a **cycle**, is reported in the window, and draws nothing. A feedback source reads the picture of the previous frame, so it may be anywhere, including the layer's own picture; its latency is exactly one frame. The first frame after it starts, after a resolution change and after a source starts again is transparent, because there is no previous picture.
5. **GPU only.** A source is copied with `glBlitFramebuffer` into a target of the same format (RGBA8 or RGBA16F) and read by the user with another blit. Nothing is read back to the processor. When the sizes differ (a layer at the reduced resolution used by a layer at full size, or the other way) the blit is linear, otherwise it is exact (`checkSurfaces` decides).
6. **A plan per frame and deck.** Before a deck is drawn, the engine lists which layers play route clips and which sender sources are chosen, and `makePlan` returns which slots to copy, which to keep for one more frame (feedback), how many users each has and which source layers must be drawn even if they are faded out (a layer with opacity 0 is normally skipped, which would make its routed picture empty). The copy happens at the point where the picture exists: after the layer chain, after the layer is composited (groups), after the master chain.
7. **Lifetime and memory.** A slot has two targets only while a feedback source needs the previous frame; otherwise one. A copy is kept for 90 frames after its last use and then released (so switching a route clip off and on again does not allocate every time), all copies are released when the resolution changes or the targets are destroyed, and the sum is limited to 512 MiB; over it a source is not copied and the log says so once. The copies count in the graphics memory estimate (`GpuMemory::routing`). One copy costs width x height x 4 bytes (RGBA8): 8,294,400 bytes (7.9 MiB, shown as 7.9 MB in the program) at 1920 x 1080, twice that for a feedback source.
8. **Two decks.** Each of the two deck passes of a transition has its own copies, so the outgoing deck keeps its routes while it fades out. Senders see the incoming deck only. A frozen outgoing deck (a second switch during a transition) draws no routes because it is a still picture.
9. **Senders can choose a source.** Spout, NDI and the recording each have a choice of Master, Layer 1 to 4 or Layers 1-2, 1-3, 1-4 (Window Output, Video routing). Master sends the output as before (the same texture, no copy). Another source is sent as an opaque copy at the output size (the existing final pass with a neutral grade, so the alpha is 1) and is made only while that sender is running. A sender whose source is not playing sends black and the window says so. The choice is saved in `settings.json` (`senderSpout`, `senderNdi`, `senderRecord`) only when it is not Master, so a profile that never used it is unchanged. Output windows keep showing the master.
10. **A window to see it.** Output, Video routing shows the eight sources with a live preview (160 x 90, refreshed every second frame, drawn only while the window is open), their size, number of users and memory, the route clips that the layers play now with their state (Working, Feedback one frame late, Blocked because it would be a loop, Source not playing), the choice for the three senders and the memory used.
11. **Off by default and invisible when unused.** Nothing is allocated, copied or drawn without a route clip or a chosen sender source (`--routing-selftest` checks that the capture counter stays 0 and the memory stays 0). With a route clip the cost is one blit per source and one per user.
12. **Library and tools.** Route clips have a dark placeholder thumbnail (they have no program to draw), are skipped by the benchmarks, and show in the browser with the badges Route and Feedback and the filter Routing; the web interface lists them as kind `route`; the show check treats them as built in.

## Alternatives considered
- **A free node graph with ports and cables.** Needs an editor, a file format, rules for the saved decks and a way to show it on a phone; the task allowed to extend what exists. A route clip is saved, copied, recalled by deck, triggered by MIDI and OSC and shown in the clip grid like every other clip. Not built; the plan and the window already show the graph that results.
- **Layer groups of your own choice.** The groups are the cumulative mix of layers 1 to n because that is what the compositor holds at that moment for free. A group of layers 2 and 3 would need its own accumulation and a place in the file. Not built.
- **Reading the picture back through the processor.** Would add a stall per source and a copy over the bus. Rejected; the blit stays on the card.
- **Sharing one texture instead of copying.** A layer's target is reused by the next layer and the compositor ping-pongs between two targets, so a shared texture would change under the user. A copy is the only safe form without restructuring `renderDeck`. The cost is one blit.
- **Cycles solved by an extra frame of delay automatically.** Would hide a wiring mistake and make timing depend on layer order. A loop is refused and shown; the user chooses the Feedback source on purpose.
- **Routing per output window.** The output windows draw from the same texture as the senders but are managed by `OutputManager` with their own slices, masks and warp; routing there needs a decision about which slice shows what. Not built.

## Consequences
- New files `core/routing.*`, `render/routing_engine.cpp`, `app/ui_routing.cpp`, `app/routing_selftest.*`, `tests/test_routing.cpp`; a new `ClipKind::RouteSource`; the engine gets `routeBytes`, `routeSlot`, `routePlan`, `setSenderSource`, `routedTexture`; new start options `--routing-selftest [FRAMES]` and `--routing-demo`; a menu entry Output, Video routing; three new optional settings; 26 new translated texts (seven languages, 2,422 keys in total).
- Without a route clip and with every sender on Master the picture, the settings file and the memory are as before.
- A faded out layer that is used as a source is drawn although it is invisible, so it costs what it costs. The window shows which layers are drawn only for routing.

## Validation / open questions
- Done here, on Linux with the Mesa software renderer (OpenGL 4.5 core context): `nvj_routing_tests` (34,049 checks), `--routing-selftest` (51 checks in the real engine), the whole suite of 44 tests, the existing self tests and the shader editor UI test, the translation check, a screenshot of the window.
- **Not done:** a real graphics card (the cost of the blits and of the sender copy at 1080p and 4K was only seen from Mesa), Windows beyond the CI build, a show with all four layers routed for hours, a recording of a routed source to a file (the recorder is handed the sender texture, but no recording was run with a routed source), OBS and NDI receivers looking at a routed sender.
- **Open for the maintainer:** whether a source should also be offered to the output windows, whether layer groups of your own choice are wanted, whether the first frame of a feedback source should show the layer's current picture instead of transparent, and whether Master should be captured after the final grade for senders that ask for it.
