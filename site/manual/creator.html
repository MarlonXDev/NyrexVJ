The Clip Creator

Chapter 18 of 38 · 12 min read

On this pageWhat it isThe three columnsObjects, materials and animationCameras and skyShared 3D worldsGeometry graphsTemplates, saving and using

#What it is

The Clip Creator builds your own 3D scenes from shapes, models and lights. You let everything move with the music and save the result as a portable .nyxclip file. The scene then plays like any other clip. Open it from the Creator menu, Open Clip Creator.

#The three columns

  • The left column lists the objects, the lights and the scene settings. Add object offers Sphere, Cube, Cylinder, Cone, Plane, Torus, Torus Knot and Model from the library. Add light gives a Directional light or a Point light, up to four.
  • The middle shows a live preview. Drag to orbit, use the mouse wheel to zoom. Free camera, Simulate audio and Reset camera help you look around, and Use this view as the clip camera keeps the view you found.
  • The right column is the inspector for the selected object. It has Transform (position, rotation, scale), Repeat (a ring or a line of copies), Material, Texture, Animation, Audio reactions and the settings for cameras and the sky.

#Objects, materials and animation

A material has a colour, Metallic, Roughness, a glow colour and strength, and optional texture, normal map and glow map. Tick Follow the layer colors to let the object take the colours of the layer it plays on, so one scene can be recoloured with one click. Animation offers movements such as Spin, Bob, Orbit, Pulse and Sway, each with its own settings like speed, height or radius.

Under Audio reactions each object has four rows. A row has a tick box, a source and a target. The source is Bass, Mid, High, Level, Kick, Beat (a pulse that follows the beat clock, so it also works with tap tempo and without sound) or one of eight Bands that split the spectrum from deep to high. The target is Size, Glow, Twist or Lift, or one that changes the material: Colour shift turns the hue (1.00 is one whole turn, so 0.33 turns red into green), Roughness makes the surface duller or shinier, Texture shift X and Y slide the texture, Inflate pushes the surface out along its normal and Ripple sends a wave over it. Rows on the same target add up, and a child object takes what its parent got. The music can also move the light intensity and the camera: zoom, orbit, tilt, roll, field of view and the point it looks at.

Colour shift, Roughness and Texture shift change what the shader draws; Inflate and Ripple move the points of the shape and keep the old normals, so a deep ripple on a sharp shape is lit as if it were flat. A texture that is shifted by a whole number lands where it started. With Simulate audio ticked the preview plays a fixed pretend music, so the same moment always gives the same picture, and a saved scene keeps its rows. A scene that uses only the old sources, the old targets and two rows is saved as before and opens in older programs; one that uses more needs 0.68.0 or newer. A row with a source or target the program does not know is left out and named in the warnings under the preview. The switch Music moves 3D materials under Settings, Performance turns the six material targets off; Size, Glow, Twist, Lift, Light and Zoom keep following the music.

Parent. Each object has a Parent drop-down, and the list shows children indented under their parent. A child moves, turns and scales with its parent and takes the music reactions its parent got on top of its own, so a small object can ride on a big one. Objects nest up to 8 levels, and the drop-down offers only objects that would not make a loop. Deleting a parent moves its children up one level; Duplicate copies the object without its children. When two objects use the same model the model is stored and uploaded once. A scene that uses a parent is saved as version 4 and needs 0.70.0 or newer; one that does not is saved as before.

#Cameras and sky

A scene can hold up to eight cameras. Pick the one that plays, or let the clip cut between all of them every few beats with Cut (beats). A camera has a distance, a height angle, a start angle, a field of view, an orbit speed and a height sway. Roll turns the picture around the line of sight. Move X, Y and Z move the point the camera looks at, in scene radii, and the camera goes with it. Shot length (beats) keeps this camera for its own number of beats; 0 uses Cut (beats). Sky and exposure set the colours of the sky, the horizon and the ground, the sky brightness and the exposure.

Glide. With Glide into the next camera above 0, the camera does not jump at a cut. Over the last part of each shot, 1.00 being the whole shot, it moves to the pose of the next camera, with the Smooth or the Linear curve (the same curves as the keys of the Show timeline). 0 is the old hard cut. A glide follows the beat clock like the cuts do, so it also works with tap tempo and MIDI clock.

Music on the camera. The target list of a camera row has Zoom, Orbit, Tilt, Roll, Field of view and Move X, Y and Z. A value of 1.00 turns the camera 90 degrees around the scene (Orbit), tilts it 45 degrees (Tilt), rolls it 45 degrees (Roll), widens the lens by 30 degrees (Field of view) or moves the point it looks at by half a scene radius (Move). The height angle stays between plus and minus 85 degrees and the field of view between 15 and 110.

Cameras from a model. If a model of the scene has cameras, they are listed under Cameras in the models. Use camera adds one as a camera of the scene, with the field of view of the file. It is the pose the file's camera has at rest, relative to the middle and size of the whole scene; an animation of the camera is not played, it does not follow the model if you move the model later, and a camera that is not a perspective one cannot be used.

On the layer. For a scene clip the Clip window has a Camera section with Camera orbit (plus and minus 180 degrees), Camera tilt (plus and minus 60) and Camera field of view (plus and minus 30). They add to the camera of the scene and are control targets (layer/N/camera-orbit, camera-tilt, camera-fov), so MIDI, OSC, the web interface and the lanes of the Show timeline can drive them; a camera move over time is a lane with keys. 0 leaves the camera alone.

The switch Camera moves in 3D scenes under Settings, Performance turns all of this off: no glide, roll, look-at offset or shot length, and the camera targets and the three layer controls are ignored. Cuts and Zoom keep working. A scene that uses none of it is saved as before and opens in older programs; one that uses it needs 0.69.0 or newer. This is a virtual camera that moves inside the scene; NyrexVJ does not follow a real, physical camera.

#Shared 3D worlds

Normally every 3D layer draws its own scene, and the upper layer covers the lower one. In a shared world the layers are drawn into one space with one depth buffer, so their objects hide each other where they overlap, with the correct one in front, whichever layer is on top. Each layer keeps its own scene, its own animation and its own music reactions, and keeps its effects, blend mode and opacity, because it is still drawn into its own picture.

In the Clip window of a scene clip the section Shared 3D world has a World choice: Own scene (the old way) or World 1 to 4. Put the layers that should meet in the same world. The lowest visible layer of the world is the owner: its camera, lights and sky are used by all of them. The other layers are placed relative to it with World position X, Y and Z (in sizes of the owner, minus 4 to 4), World turn (minus 180 to 180 degrees) and World size (0.1 to 4; 1 fits the layer to the size of the owner). The owner can be moved the same way relative to the camera. The four position and turn sliders are control targets (layer/N/world-x, world-y, world-z, world-yaw), so MIDI, OSC, the web interface, macros and the Show timeline can move a layer through the world.

Some things to know. A translucent surface blends with what is behind it inside its own layer only, not with the objects of another layer. A layer that is hidden or bypassed, a scene that is still loading and a layer with another resolution than the owner draw on their own. A layer that is not the owner ignores its own camera and Zoom, because a world has one view. Scenes made in the Clip Creator and glTF models use the lights of the owner; the classic-looking built-in scenes keep their own lights.

The switch Shared 3D worlds under Settings, Performance turns it off: every layer draws its own scene again, whatever world it is set to. A preset only stores the world values of a layer when they are not the default. An example is in docs/examples/shared-3d-world: an arm and a cube, two clips that overlap in space, and a preset that puts them in World 1.

#Geometry graphs

A geometry graph builds a 3D shape in a few steps instead of taking one of the built-in shapes: start with a shape, roughen it, twist it, repeat it in a ring and let the music swell it. It lives in the Clip Creator and is a graph of its own, not the node editor for effects.

Add object, Geometry graph adds an object that shows a graph. Pick one in the Graph drop-down or press New graph; Duplicate graph and Delete graph do what they say, and the Graph name can be changed. Below that the graph is a list of nodes. Every row has a kind, one or two inputs (the nodes that come before it) and its values; Add node adds a row and Remove node takes one away. The input drop-down offers only nodes that would not make a loop. A line under the list shows the vertices, triangles and instances of the result and the time it took. Objects that use the same graph and have a Material node in it share one mesh.

The kinds of node:

  • Primitive is the start, one of the built-in shapes.
  • Subdivide splits every triangle, 1 to 4 levels. Every level makes about four times as many triangles.
  • Transform moves, turns and scales the shape. A negative scale mirrors it and the shape stays solid.
  • Noise pushes the surface in and out with 3D noise: Amount (relative to the size of the shape), Frequency, Octaves (1 to 4) and Seed. It works best after Subdivide, because a coarse shape has few points to move.
  • Twist turns the shape around the vertical axis, more the higher it is (degrees per unit).
  • Merge joins two shapes into one (the second input is used).
  • Instance repeats the shape as a Ring, Line, Grid or Scatter, up to 1,024 times. Count, Radius, Spacing and Seed set the layout and Size variation makes each copy a little bigger or smaller. Everything before an Instance is one shape, so Merge has to come before it, and an Instance cannot be repeated again.
  • Material sets the colour, Metallic, Roughness, glow and Follow the layer colors for the result. Without it the material of the object is used.
  • Audio adds one music reaction (Source, Reaction and Amount); a graph can have up to four. The reactions you can pick are the ones that move the shape in the shader or as a whole: Size, Glow, Twist, Lift, Colour shift, Roughness, Texture shift X and Y, Inflate and Ripple. The copies of an Instance follow them together.
  • Output is the end. There must be exactly one.

The graph is worked out once, when the scene loads, and the result is kept, so the music never builds it again. That is why the music reactions are limited to what the shader can do. Changing a value in the editor works the graph out again.

A graph has limits: 48 nodes, 200,000 vertices, 1,000,000 indices, 1,024 instances and 4,000,000 triangles drawn per frame (the triangles of the shape times the instances). Every instance is its own draw call, so a thousand instances of a detailed shape are heavy; use few triangles for the shape you repeat. If a graph breaks a limit, loops back on itself, has no Output or a missing input, the object is left out, the message under the preview names the node, and the rest of the scene plays.

The switch Geometry graphs under Settings, Performance turns the feature off for scenes that load from then on: objects with a graph are left out and the warnings say so. A scene that uses a graph is saved as version 5 and needs 0.71.0 or newer; one that does not is saved as before. An example is in docs/examples/geometry-graph: a ring of 14 rocks that grows with the kick, and a twisted knot that swells with the bass.

#Templates, saving and using

Start from a template (Creator menu, Start from a template, or Open in the window) or from an empty scene with New. Save writes a .nyxclip file into the clips folder. It contains the scene description and, as copies, the .glb models and the PNG or JPEG textures the scene uses, up to 200 MiB, so one file is all you need to share a scene. It then shows up in the Library under Scenes as Scene: name. Use as clip also puts it into the selected cell of the deck. Save copy saves the scene under a new name.

Only .glb models can be stored in a scene. A .gltf that needs separate files is listed in the Assets view but cannot be used. Undo and Redo work inside the Creator while it has the focus. An unsaved scene is kept in the recovery folder in case the program closes unexpectedly.
