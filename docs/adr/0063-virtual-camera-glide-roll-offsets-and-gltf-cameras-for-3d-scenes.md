# ADR 0063: Virtual camera for 3D scenes: glide between cameras, roll, look-at offset, shot lengths, camera controls and glTF cameras

- **Status:** Accepted for the camera moves, the glide, the shot lengths, the seven camera targets, the three layer controls, the import of a model camera and the switch, requested by MarlonXDev (2026-11-27 20:32, prompt 40): a virtual camera that can be steered, moved and cut between smoothly, driven by the music and the existing automation, with imported glTF cameras where the architecture allows it. Real camera tracking is **not built**; it is an open question (below).
- **Date:** 2026-11-28
- **Owners:** Maintainers (pending assignment)
- **Related:** [SCENE3D.md](../SCENE3D.md), [INTERFACES.md](../INTERFACES.md), [PERFORMANCE.md](../PERFORMANCE.md), [TIMELINE.md](../TIMELINE.md), [TESTING.md](../TESTING.md), [ADR 0004](0004-scene-document-and-clip-pack.md) (the scene document and the clip pack), [ADR 0006](0006-clip-creator.md) (the editor), [ADR 0025](0025-3d-engine-2.md) (glTF lights and cameras were read, not applied), [ADR 0031](0031-show-timeline.md) (the keyframe engine, reused), [ADR 0062](0062-audio-reactive-3d-materials-sources-and-targets-for-scene-bindings.md) (the bindings this adds targets to)

## Context
- State at main `278f393` (0.68.0): a scene has up to eight cameras. A camera is a distance, a height angle, a start angle, a field of view, an orbit speed, a height sway and bindings (Zoom only). With Cut (beats) above 0 the clip jumps to the next camera every that many beats, always as a hard cut. The camera always looks at the middle of the scene and the picture is always upright. The layer Scale zooms.
- Cameras of a glTF file were read into `Scene::fileCameras` (up to 16) and shown as a count; nothing used them (ADR 0025).
- The Show timeline already has keyframe lanes with Hold, Linear and Smooth shapes for any control target (`evaluateKeys`, `ControlSystem::fireTarget`). MIDI, OSC, the web interface and the timeline all reach a layer through the control targets (`layer/N/...`).
- Not part of this change, by the wording of the request: the timeline itself (prompt 08), the 3D import (prompt 04) and image or object tracking (prompt 28). A second keyframe engine was ruled out.

## Decision
1. **A camera gets a roll, a look-at offset and a shot length.** Roll turns the picture around the line of sight (plus and minus 180 degrees). Move X, Y and Z move the point the camera looks at, in units of the scene radius (plus and minus 4), so the camera follows it. The shot length is a number of beats (0 uses Cut (beats)). Pose, field of view, orbit and dolly (the existing Distance) stay as they were.
2. **Glide between cameras.** `Scene::cameraGlide` (0 to 1, the share of each shot used to move to the next camera) and `cameraGlideShape` (Smooth or Linear; the editor offers both). 0 is the old hard cut. The curve is the one of the Show timeline: `easeAlong(KeyShape, u)` was split out of `evaluateKeys`, so there is one curve in the program. The blend is made from poses, not from pictures: yaw and roll go the short way round, the distance changes by a ratio, field of view, height angle and look-at offset change linearly. `cameraShotAt(scene, beat, glide)` names the camera of the beat, the next one and the blend; `cameraPoseAt` computes the pose.
3. **One pose, one view.** `cameraPoseOf` turns a camera, the time and the audio into a pose; `cameraView` turns a pose into an eye, a target, an up vector, a field of view and a distance. The expressions are written in the order the old renderer used, so a scene that does not use the new features gives the same floats as before (tested bit for bit against the old formulas, and by comparing the pictures of a 0.68.0 build, see Measured).
4. **Seven camera targets for the audio bindings** (the camera rows had Zoom only): **Orbit** (amount 1 is 90 degrees around the scene), **Tilt** (1 is 45 degrees up), **Roll** (1 is 45 degrees), **Field of view** (1 is 30 degrees), **Move X, Y, Z** (1 is half a scene radius). Every limit is applied after the sum: tilt to plus and minus 85 degrees, field of view to 15 to 110 degrees, look-at offset to plus and minus 4, and a value that is not a number counts as 0.
5. **Three layer controls for the existing automation.** `layer/N/camera-orbit` (plus and minus 180 degrees), `layer/N/camera-tilt` (plus and minus 60) and `layer/N/camera-fov` (plus and minus 30) are added to the control list like `layer/N/scale`, so MIDI, OSC, the web interface, macros and the Show timeline lanes drive them without a new path. They offset the camera of whatever scene the layer plays; the default is 0, they are saved in presets only when they are not 0 (`cameraOrbit`, `cameraTilt`, `cameraFov` in the layer), and the Clip window shows them for scene clips. Keyframes are therefore the timeline's, not a second engine.
6. **glTF cameras can be used, as a choice.** When a model of the scene has perspective cameras, the Clip Creator lists them under Cameras in the models. Use camera adds one as a normal camera of the scene (`sceneCameraFromFile`): the world matrix of the camera, taken with the transform of the object, is turned into a height angle, a start angle, a distance, a roll and a look-at offset relative to the middle and size of the whole scene. It is a snapshot of the rest pose, it follows the scene and not the model, the distance is held between 0.2 and 6 and the height angle between plus and minus 85 degrees. Orthographic cameras are listed and cannot be used. Nothing is applied without the choice, so models that bring cameras look as before.
7. **The switch.** `Settings::sceneCameraMoves`, key `sceneCameraMoves`, default true, written to `settings.json` only when false (Settings, Performance, Camera moves in 3D scenes). Off draws the old camera: no glide (hard cut, as before), no roll, no look-at offset, no shot lengths, and the seven camera targets and the three layer controls are ignored; cuts and Zoom still work. The Clip Creator preview follows it.
8. **Old files stay old.** A scene is written as `"version": 1` while it uses nothing newer than 0.67.0, as `"version": 2` when it uses the 0.68.0 bindings (new sources, material targets, more than two rows), and as `"version": 3` when it uses any camera feature (roll, offset, shot length, glide, a camera target). `docRequiredVersion` decides; a program that does not know the version refuses it with its message about a newer NyrexVJ. The keys of the new fields are written only when they are not 0.
9. **Unknown values are named.** An unknown glide curve is dropped to Smooth with a note, and a camera target on an object or light (or a material target on a camera) is changed to the one that fits and said so, as for the other bindings in ADR 0062.
10. **The free camera of the editor is unchanged.** With Free camera ticked the preview ignores the camera of the scene, including roll and offset, as it ignored the rest before; the whole pose is replaced.

## Measured
All values come from `nvj_vcam_tests`, `NyrexVJ --scene-selftest tests/data/models` and `--bench-scene` on Linux with Mesa llvmpipe (software OpenGL on the processor, two cores, one machine, not a graphics card).
- Unit tests: `nvj_vcam_tests` is new, 10,145 checks, 0 failed. They cover the curve against `evaluateKeys`; the shot of every beat for uniform, timed and gliding cuts; the pose against the old formulas bit for bit with the features unused; every camera target with its limits and bad numbers; blending around the 180 degree seam; the view with roll and offset; the conversion of a file camera and back, including a scaled matrix and bad numbers; the document keys, versions 1, 2 and 3 and the refusal of 4; unknown and misplaced keys, which are named in the notes; the merge of model cameras; the layer controls and their preset round trip; a clip pack round trip; and the setting. `nvj_matbind_tests` (888 checks) was adjusted for the new version and passes; the whole ctest list (69 tests) passes.
- Render tests: the scene self test has 33 new checks and passes 79 of 79. Moving the look-at offset by one scene radius moves the centre of the picture from x 128 to x 40 (256 pixels wide); up and down move it in opposite ways; a roll of 90 degrees turns a 59 x 128 picture into a 128 x 59 one with the same 2,390 lit pixels, and a half turn mirrors it through the centre. An Orbit binding, a Tilt binding, a Field of view binding and the three layer controls give the same picture as a camera placed there (mean difference 0.00). A hard cut jumps by a mean 3.16 of 255 per pixel at the cut and a glide by 0.00; a linear glide in the middle of a shot gives the picture of a camera halfway between the two. With the switch off a glide, a roll, an offset, a shot length, a binding and a layer control change nothing while the cuts stay. A camera of a model file with two cameras is found, imported, and then shows the test model at x 128 to 209 and y 45 to 126, where the file's camera predicts about 128 to 211 and a height of about 83. Two players with the same cameras, music and beats give the same camera.
- Same picture as 0.68.0: the self test of a 0.68.0 build (built from `278f393` with only the hash lines added) and of this version, same models, same machine: the pictures of the 7 built-in scenes at 3 times, in the editor path and in the camera path, and the 12 music moments of the material test have the same hashes, byte for byte.
- Mutation probes (a fault put in, then reverted): 10 faults for the unit tests (among them the offset limit on x and on y and the tilt limit left out) and 2 for the renderer (among them the free preview camera keeping the roll of the scene). Two of them were **not** caught at first, the offset limit on x and the roll of the free camera; a check was added for each and then all 12 were caught.
- Cost: working out a pose and a view with 8 cameras, a glide and 3 bindings takes 0.148 to 0.160 microseconds (best of 5 rounds of 20,000, three runs). The frame time at 256 x 256 with 8 cameras, three runs: 0.80, 1.10 and 0.84 ms with hard cuts, 0.79, 1.00 and 0.88 ms with the switch off and 0.74, 0.82 and 0.86 ms with a glide and bindings, so no mode is above the others beyond the noise of the machine. `--bench-scene` on the same machine, 0.68.0 against this version, average ms per frame: Torus Knot Mesh 154.9 and 154.5, Chrome Orbs Mesh 250.8 and 253.2, about 1 % apart. The estimated graphics memory is the same (58,290,528 bytes): the camera is a matrix, so there is no new texture, buffer, thread or readback. The far plane is moved out by the length of the look-at offset so a moved camera does not clip the scene.
- By hand in the real window (Xvfb, software OpenGL): the camera page with the new sliders, the target list of a camera row, Add object, a model from the library, the camera list of a model with the Use camera button, and the Camera section of the Clip window for a scene clip were opened and looked at.

## Not done
- **Real camera tracking.** Everything here is a virtual camera: it moves in the scene. Following a real, physical camera (a tracker, a head, a handheld rig) needs a decision on the hardware and the protocol (for example FreeD, OSC from a tracker or VRPN), on lens calibration and on the latency to accept, and none of that can be tested without the hardware. The layer controls can already be driven from outside through OSC, MIDI or the web interface, so a tracker program could send them, but nothing reads a tracker and the result is not a calibrated match to a real lens. Nothing was built on a guess.
- **Camera animation inside a model file** (a glTF camera on an animated node) and **orthographic cameras.** An imported camera is its rest pose.
- **Keyframes inside the scene.** There are none; the Show timeline lanes on the layer controls are the keyframes, and the cuts, shot lengths and glide are the sequence. A free path (a spline through poses) was not built.
- **Roll, look-at offset and dolly as layer controls.** The layer controls cover orbit, tilt and field of view; Dolly is the existing layer Scale and a camera's Distance. They can be added the same way.
- **Gliding to a camera chosen by hand.** Choosing another camera in the editor or by script cuts at once; the glide runs only between the cameras of the cut sequence.
- **A real graphics card, Windows at run time (built in CI only), a long show, a tracker, and the sliders of the new page dragged by hand.** See `M-VCM-1`.

## Open question for the maintainer
- Should NyrexVJ follow a real camera, and if so which tracker or protocol, with which lens calibration and how much latency is acceptable? It is a hardware and calibration decision and the answer changes what is built (a protocol reader, a calibration step, a latency budget).

## Consequences
- `Settings` has one new field and `settings.json` one optional key; older programs ignore it.
- The control list has twelve more ids (`layer/1..4/camera-orbit`, `camera-tilt`, `camera-fov`) after `layer/N/scale`. No id moved or was renamed, so OSC, MIDI and the Unreal plugin keep working. The 64 bit schema hash of the network sync is made from the ids and has changed, so a master and a follower on different versions around this release only exchange the clock until both are updated.
- A scene that uses no camera feature is written exactly as before. A scene that does is version 3, and 0.68.0 and older refuse it with the existing message.
- A preset only has the three layer keys when one is not 0.
- Not run on a real graphics card or on Windows beyond the CI build; see `M-VCM-1`.
